ENTERPRISE GEOMETRY REVIEW
Date: November 11, 2026
Model reviewed: SECOND MODEL / movie-reference Enterprise
Reference: supplied top, bottom, starboard, front and back orthographic sheet.
The supplied sheet matches assets/reference/image-1444/enterprise-orthos.png.

Scope and method
----------------
Compared fresh orthographic renders of the current model with all five supplied
views, and inspected world-space mesh bounds and the geometry-building code.
High-detail textures were used for inspection, with the shuttle-bay doors closed.
This is an assessment only. No simulation code, geometry or assets were changed.

Reference limitations: the drawn projections are not perfectly identical in
scale. The saucer spans roughly 419 pixels in the normalized side view but about
408-410 pixels in some other views. A single global overlay therefore creates
an apparent 2-3% size difference. Treat small differences cautiously and compare
each view's proportions to its own saucer diameter. Numerical model dimensions
below use the model's 1.36-unit saucer diameter, not real-world ship dimensions.

PRIORITIZED DIFFERENCES TO REVIEW
-------------------------------

1. Aft engineering-hull outline is too flat in top and bottom views.
   Priority: high. Confidence: high.
   The sheet shows a rounded, progressively narrowing stern cap around the bay.
   The model's aft engineering body ends with a much straighter transverse edge
   and more abrupt corners. Its side-view belly scoop is present, but it does
   not reproduce the rounded plan-view stern. Review the aft cross sections and
   the transition between secondary-hull, aft fairing and bay-door frame.

2. Pylons need the reference's plan-view shaping and attachment transitions.
   Priority: high. Confidence: high for simplification; medium for exact sweep.
   The current support is built from a constant-section rectangular beam, with
   its principal length at one fore/aft station. The reference, especially the
   bottom view, shows a subtly swept/tapered outline and shaped root/engine
   junctions. Current top/bottom silhouettes read as straight rectangular bars.
   Overall placement and the diagonal front/back arrangement are already close;
   this is a local shape/attachment issue, not a reason to move both engines.

3. Nacelle fins/raised fairings are too long, and the inboard fin alignment drifts.
   Priority: high. Confidence: high for measured geometry; medium-high visually.
   Current aft fin length is 0.37837 units, or 27.82% of saucer diameter. The
   existing reference proportion is about 24.9%, making it roughly 12% longer.
   The source grows each fin by 15% after its initial station alignment.
   The inboard fin's forward edge is now at Z=-0.31768, versus pylon midpoint
   Z=-0.29300: a 0.02468-unit offset, about 7.6 pixels on a 419-pixel saucer.
   Review the inboard and aft raised fittings individually; do not shorten the
   whole nacelle to correct this local discrepancy.

4. Neck/dorsal connector is too angular at its hull junctions.
   Priority: medium-high. Confidence: high.
   The model uses an extruded four-point wedge with planar sides and abrupt
   joins. The sheet shows softer shoulders where the neck enters the saucer and
   engineering hull. The front-view exposed neck also appears more narrow and
   square-sided than the drawn transition. Preserve the broadly correct slope
   and joint stations while reviewing bevels, root width and blended shoulders.
   Exact hidden neck cross sections cannot be recovered from this sheet alone.

5. Saucer upper surface and rim have overly straight/segmented profile changes.
   Priority: medium. Confidence: high for visual profile difference.
   The model has long straight radial slopes and distinct slope breaks between
   its profile rings. The drawing has a smoother crown and more gently blended
   transitions into the rim and upper deck. The rim also reads as a stronger,
   darker wedge in current front/back renders. Darkness is a lighting issue;
   the straight silhouette transitions are the geometry issue to review.
   Keep the existing overall saucer diameter as the starting point.

6. Bridge/deck tiers look more block-like than the reference.
   Priority: medium. Confidence: medium-high.
   Current front and side views show a relatively cylindrical upper tier, a
   flat lip and a low, flat-topped sensor cap. The reference has more rounded
   shoulders and a softer stepped crown. The teardrop's plan-view width and
   length are already close; review vertical curvature, lip thickness and cap
   shape rather than enlarging the entire teardrop.

7. Underside saucer bowl needs a smoother transition into its bottom tier.
   Priority: medium. Confidence: medium.
   The reference side/front silhouette descends in a more continuous rounded
   curve toward the lower sensor structure. The current model shows a broad
   flatter bottom and a more abrupt stepped connection to the lower tier.
   Its overall bowl width is close to the existing reference proportion, so
   profile curvature and the bottom junction deserve attention before scaling.

8. Main-cylinder forward shoulder is set aft of the unmodified reference.
   Priority: medium / confirm intended revision. Confidence: high.
   The model's cylinder begins at world Z=-1.00950, approximately X=1134 on
   the project's normalized side-view registration. The supplied sheet's
   original main-cylinder forward landmark is X=1145, a difference of roughly
   11 pixels, or 2.6% of the saucer diameter. The current verification code
   explicitly allows this previously revised shoulder position. This is an
   intentional existing deviation, not evidence of a newly broken mesh.

9. Deflector dish appears small inside its surrounding collar.
   Priority: medium / verify against a close-up. Confidence: medium.
   Current dish diameter is 0.17460 units against a 0.26000-unit collar, a ratio
   of about 67%. The reference front view appears to fill more of the collar
   with the reflector/ring assembly. Confirm which visible reference rings are
   raised structure versus material/paint before changing the dish diameter.
   The dark current reflector should not itself be treated as a geometry error.

10. Aft bay surround and lower sill are more pronounced than the reference.
    Priority: medium. Confidence: medium-high.
    The rear render shows a more strongly projecting, segmented canopy/frame
    and heavier lower threshold. The reference presents a cleaner rounded arch
    within a smoother outer stern. Review this together with item 1; altering
    just the doors would leave the surrounding hull discrepancy unresolved.
    Interior garage layout is not assessable from these exterior drawings.

11. Impulse housing face is close, but its fairing integration is simplified.
    Priority: low-medium. Confidence: medium.
    Twin outlets and the central port are present, and their broad arrangement
    resembles the rear view. The model's capsule face and its junction to the
    saucer are more mechanically straight and sharply defined. The drawing
    suggests softer end fairings and a more smoothly integrated center spine.
    Review junction curvature before changing outlet spacing or total width.

WHAT ALREADY LOOKS CLOSE
-----------------------
- Saucer plan form is circular and the overall primary-hull size is reasonable.
- Nacelle separation: 0.878 / 1.36 = 64.56% of saucer diameter, close to the sheet.
- Overall nacelle length: about 1.63967 / 1.36 = 120.56%, close to the side view.
- Main engineering-hull height: about 22.35% of saucer diameter, broadly close.
- Engine elevation, collector position relative to the saucer, and basic
  diagonal pylon layout are broadly consistent with the supplied views.
- Upper teardrop footprint: width 20.33% and length 28.32% of saucer diameter,
  close to the project's existing reference proportions.
- Underside bowl width: about 42.65% of saucer diameter; no strong evidence here
  for a major global width change.
- The neck's general rake, twin nacelle symmetry, deflector/probe arrangement,
  twin impulse outlets, underside sensor and clamshell-bay concept are present.

NOT GEOMETRY FINDINGS
--------------------
The darker underside, dark deflector, mottled collectors, hull color, window
visibility, registry placement and panel lines are material, lighting, texture
or animation matters. They were not counted as geometric defects in this list.
The supplied sheet cannot establish internal engine construction, garage space,
hidden intersections, moving-door clearance or exact surface fillet radii.

Suggested order if changes are later authorized: aft engineering hull/bay
surround, pylons and fins, neck transitions, saucer/deck/bowl curvature, then
deflector and impulse details. Recheck all five views after each major group.
